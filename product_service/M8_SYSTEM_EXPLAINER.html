What is M8?

M8 is a product recommendation engine that answers:

"Given this farmer's crop, pest problem, urgency, and history — which 2 products should we recommend, and why?"

It is NOT a simple lookup table. It balances:

🌿 Efficacy

Does this product actually treat the problem?

📊 Adoption

Do farmers in this region actually buy it?

📦 Availability

Is it in stock at nearby retailers?

Plus critical agronomic intelligence: resistance management, treatment intent matching, and contraindication checks.

System Architecture (3 Layers + Post-Processing)

Layer 1: Agronomic Efficacy (40–60% weight) → Does it work?
↓
Layer 2: Behavioral Adoption (15–30% weight) → Will they buy it?
↓
Layer 3: Availability + Affordability (15–25% weight) → Can they get it?
↓
Post-Processing: Resistance Mgmt + Diversity + Contraindications

Key Design Decision: Dynamic Weighting

Weights shift with urgency. High urgency → efficacy dominates (farmer needs the RIGHT product NOW). Low urgency → adoption and affordability matter more (farmer has time to choose).

Urgency Efficacy Adoption Availability
Low (0.1) 42% 29% 29%
Medium (0.5) 50% 23% 28%
High (0.9) 58% 17% 25%

Layer 1: Agronomic Efficacy (The Core)

This is NOT popularity. This is: "How well does this product actually treat the problem?"

efficacy = 0.25×crop_match + 0.30×pest_match + 0.15×stage_match + 0.20×efficacy_rating + 0.10×intent_alignment
Component Weight What it measures
Pest Match 30% Does this product target the specific pest/disease?
Crop Match 25% Is this product registered for this crop?
Efficacy Rating 20% Domain expert rating of product effectiveness
Stage Match 15% Is this product effective at the current growth stage?
Intent Alignment 10% Curative for high urgency, preventive for low urgency

Treatment Intent Classification

🛡️ Preventive

Apply before symptoms. Low urgency. Kavach, Cruiser, Vibrance.

💊 Curative

Apply after early symptoms. Medium-high urgency. Score, Tilt, Alto.

🚨 Rescue

Emergency intervention. Critical urgency. Movondo, Actara.

🌱 Seed Treatment

Pre-sowing only. Cruiser, Vibrance Integral.

Layer 2: Behavioral Adoption (ML from POS Data)

Learned from 235,000+ real retail transactions. Answers: "What do farmers in this region actually buy for this crop?"

How it's trained

  • Crop→Product affinity: Which products sell most in tehsils where a specific crop is grown?
  • District→Product popularity: Regional purchase preferences (Jaipur buys different products than Ludhiana)
  • Temporal split: Train on Oct–Feb POS, validate on Mar–Apr purchases
  • No synthetic data: All signals from real transactions

Critical distinction: Adoption ≠ Efficacy. A product can be popular but not the best choice for a specific pest. That's why efficacy dominates the scoring.

Resistance Management (MoA Tracking)

This is what separates M8 from a simple lookup table. Real agronomy requires Mode of Action rotation to prevent pest resistance.

How it works

Same product used in last 14 days → 60% penalty (severe)
Same MoA group used recently → 35% penalty (moderate)
Same MoA used 3+ times this season → 50% penalty + advisory generated

MoA Groups in the Catalog

Group Class Products

Diversity enforcement: Top-K recommendations always contain different MoA groups. This prevents recommending Score + Tilt + Alto (all FRAC-3 triazoles) together.

"Why Not Recommended" — Rejection Explainability

The system doesn't just say what to use. It explains why alternatives were rejected. This builds trust.

Example rejection explanations

Score 250 EC "Same MoA group (FRAC-3) used recently — rotate to prevent resistance"
Movondo "Premium pricing — may not suit small farm budget"
Cruiser 350 FS "Seed treatment not applicable at flowering growth stage"
Kavach 75 WP "Low stock availability in your district"

Contraindication Checks

Safety rules that override scoring:

Rule Penalty Rationale
Foliar spray within 14 days of harvest 70% penalty Pre-harvest interval (PHI) food safety
Seed treatment at flowering/pod stage 85% penalty Wrong application timing — ineffective
Out of stock (availability < 10%) 70% penalty Don't recommend unavailable products

Evaluation Methodology

How do you test a recommendation system without explicit "correct answer" labels?

Implicit Feedback Evaluation

Use actual purchase behavior as ground truth. If a farmer in crop X, district Y bought product Z — would our ranker have recommended Z?

Metrics

Metric Value Interpretation
Hit Rate @2 18.8% Purchased product in top-2 (random=16.7%)
Hit Rate @5 43.8% Purchased product in top-5 (random=41.7%)
MRR 0.213 Mean Reciprocal Rank
Coverage 75% 9/12 products get recommended
MoA Diversity 1.00 Perfect — every top-3 has unique MoA groups
Lift over random 1.13× 13% better than random selection

Why modest lift is expected (and acceptable)

  • Only 12 products — random baseline is already 16.7% for top-2. Ceiling is low.
  • Evaluation uses pest=general — POS data doesn't tell us what pest the farmer faced. The ranker's biggest strength (pest-specific matching) can't be tested from purchases alone.
  • The real value isn't hit rate — it's agronomic coherence, resistance management, explainability, and contextual adaptation. These can't be measured by purchase prediction.
